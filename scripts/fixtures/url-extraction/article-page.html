What Changed in Review Practice

Posted in Engineering Notes

Code review used to be a gate placed at the end of the work. A change was finished, then someone else decided whether it could ship. That framing produced long queues and reviews that arrived far too late to influence the design of the change they were reviewing.

The shift was to treat review as a conversation that starts while the work is still malleable. See the latency report for the measurements that prompted the change, and the queue depth study for the follow up.

What We Changed

Three adjustments accounted for most of the improvement.

  1. Reviews were requested from a draft rather than a finished branch.
  2. Any change over four hundred lines was split before review was requested.
  3. Reviewers were assigned by area of ownership instead of by round robin.

What We Measured

The metrics that moved were narrow, and the ones that did not move are worth stating plainly.

  • Time from request to first substantive comment fell sharply.
  • Total review comments per change stayed roughly flat.
  • Revert rate did not change in either direction.

A review that arrives after the author has moved on is documentation, not collaboration.

What We Would Do Differently

The size limit did most of the work and the ownership routing did the rest. If we ran the experiment again we would introduce the size limit first and measure it alone for a full quarter before changing anything else.
